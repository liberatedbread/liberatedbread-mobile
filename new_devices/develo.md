
4.5.1 Entire estate is rebadged Z-Wave (Danfoss/Philio/POPP); €5–22 hardware; no dedicated German rescue service exists
Devolo is the cleanest EU distress case: devolo's official 2025-08-21 press release announced Home Control servers would switch off 2025-12-31, and they did; the app was removed from stores the same day. What survives is autopilot only — pre-programmed schedules and if/then rules execute locally, but no reconfiguration, remote access, app, or LAN UI, since even local control always routed through the cloud portal. The mydevolo account backend still runs and is the sole thing keeping the Home Assistant gateway integration alive (local API, cloud-issued credentials); HA's alert recommends manual migration to Z-Wave JS, noting no mydevolo shutdown date "but there is also no guarantee". The only official successor, ROCKETHOME, migrates the hub into its own subscription cloud at €99/yr plus ~€30 activation — rules and scenes not migrated, whitelist full of gaps.
The rescuable scope is essentially the whole estate. All ~14 device SKUs are standard Z-Wave Plus EU slaves under manufacturer ID 0x0175, with OEM mapping documented via Z-Wave Alliance certification records and the openHAB database: the radiator thermostat is a Danfoss LC-13 variant, the metering plug a Philio PAN11, motion/contact sensors Philio PST02-family, the siren a Philio PH-PSE02, wall switch and key fob POPP/Z-Wave.me rebadges. Rescue is exclude-then-re-include to Z-Wave JS, Homey, openHAB, homee, or Hubitat — no firmware work. Difficulty is medium: every device needs physical re-pairing (the smoke detector's exclusion dance and thermostat battery-reset are documented but fiddly), automations are rebuilt from scratch, and target hardware runs ~€120–180 (HA Green plus ZWA-2) or €399 (Homey Pro). The sole e-waste item is the MT2600 hub — a closed gateway reusable only via ROCKETHOME or the mydevolo-dependent stopgap.
Install base is the flagged research gap: no official figure exists; "several thousand" systems sold in the 2015 launch year per a government-backed brochure, then a decade of MediaMarkt/Amazon/Conrad distribution across DE/AT/CH/Benelux/UK — an inference of tens of thousands of households. Used-market evidence is direct and dated: hubs at €10 typical (one listing literally free), thermostats €5–10 used and €15–22 new-in-box, starter packs €25–80, lots surging June–July 2026, prices still falling since September 2025. Demand is German-language and active: a homee migration thread with 4,300 views; Homey threads begging over a year for siren and flood-sensor drivers; at least four substantive German migration guides published August–December 2025 — but no dedicated devolo rescue service in any language. Monetization gap: a German-language migration product — per-device reset cheat-sheets and videos, a Z-Wave JS kit undercutting Homey Pro, siren/flood-sensor driver closure, and a buy-back/refurb channel arbitraging the flooded €5–22 used market — with the estate as beachhead for underserved EU fleets. Key risk: the mydevolo backend's eventual death forces hard cutover under time pressure, while ROCKETHOME skims the non-technical majority into €99/yr lock-in and the unquantified base may prove smaller than proxies suggest.
